## 2. Legal frame (EU/PL). UNCERTAIN throughout

1. **Browsewrap vs clickwrap.** Most of the vendor ToU quoted here say they bind "by using the Website", which is browsewrap. Under EU/PL consumer and civil law, a contract needs the terms to be reasonably available and accepted. For a visitor who never saw them, and especially for terms not even linked from the page (Workday, Phenom employer domains, Mastercard, Kingfisher), being bound is **doubtful**. When the terms *are* linked in the footer (Oracle CE "Legal Notices", Siemens "Terms of use"), the argument is stronger but still uncertain. Clickwrap terms (eRecruiter candidate account, Oracle's "By clicking Agree" applicant terms) bind only people who clicked. Jobscanner never creates accounts, logs in or applies.
2. **EU Database Directive 96/9/EC, sui generis right (Art. 7). PL: ustawa o ochronie baz danych.**
   - *CV-Online Latvia v Melons*, C-762/19 (3 June 2021) is directly about job-ad meta-search. The CJEU held that a specialised search engine that *"copies and indexes the whole or a substantial part of a database freely accessible on the Internet and then allows its users to search that database on its own website… is 'extracting' and 're-utilising' that content… which may be prohibited by the maker… where those acts adversely affect its investment… namely that they constitute a risk to the possibility of redeeming that investment"*. The quote comes from the AC&R summary of the operative part (https://acr.amsterdam/en/ecj-database/c-762-19). EUR-Lex and curia blocked or timed out our direct fetch, so the text was **not re-read on EUR-Lex**: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62019CJ0762.
   - The same summary restates *BHB* (C-203/02): an investment in "obtaining" excludes *"the resources used for the creation as such of independent materials"*. **An employer's own vacancies are created, not obtained, so its career site may not even qualify for the sui generis right. UNCERTAIN.** The ATS vendor isn't the "maker" either.
   - Private-use extraction from electronic databases isn't exempt. Polish art. 8 covers non-electronic DBs only (**UNVERIFIED**, from memory of the Act). So the safeguards are: keep extraction from any one board non-substantial, or show no harm to the maker's investment. Linking back and not replacing the source both help.
3. **Ryanair v PR Aviation, C-30/14 (15 Jan 2015).** If a database has *neither* copyright *nor* sui generis protection, the Directive's user rights (Arts 6(1), 8, 15) don't apply, so the owner may restrict use **by contract**, subject to national law (EUR-Lex https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62014CJ0030, summary via search; direct fetch was JS-blocked). This is why ToU still matter even where the database right is weak, but only if a contract was actually formed (see point 1).
4. **DSM Directive 2019/790, TDM.** Art. 4 (general TDM) applies unless the use is *"expressly reserved by their rightholders in an appropriate manner, such as machine-readable means in the case of content made publicly available online"* (Art. 4(3), via https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2019/790/article/4). Art. 3 covers research organisations only and doesn't apply to us. **PL:** Copyright Act art. 26³ and Database Act art. 8a (added by Dz.U. 2024 poz. 1254, in force 2024-09-20, https://dziennikustaw.gov.pl/D2024000125401.pdf). Their wording: *"Wolno zwielokrotniać rozpowszechnione bazy danych w celu eksploracji tekstów i danych, chyba że uprawniony zastrzegł inaczej."* For content available online the reservation must be *"w formacie przeznaczonym do odczytu maszynowego … wraz z metadanymi"*.
   - **Caveat:** the PL definition of TDM is *"analiza wyłącznie przy zastosowaniu zautomatyzowanej techniki … w celu wygenerowania określonych informacji, obejmujących w szczególności wzorce, tendencje i korelacje"*. Showing individual job ads to a user is arguably **not TDM**; it is reproduction and display. So the TDM exception may not be what makes Jobscanner lawful. **UNCERTAIN.**
   - Opt-outs actually found: **SAP ToU** (*"SAP reserves the right to prohibit text and data mining of its websites… under Directive (EU) 2019/790 Article 4"*), **Siemens ToU** (TDM ban), and **careers.phenom.com** robots `Content-Signal: ai-train=no, search=yes, ai-input=no`. **`ai-input=no` matters if Jobscanner passes job text to an LLM.** No TDMRep (`/.well-known/tdmrep.json` was non-200 everywhere), no `tdm-reservation` headers, and no robots meta found on the 10 hosts checked.
   - Whether natural-language ToU count as "machine-readable" is unsettled. A German court is reported to have said yes (LAION, Hamburg 2024: **UNVERIFIED**, not re-checked).
5. **GDPR.** Job ads without people in them aren't personal data. **Recruiter or hiring-manager names, e-mails and photos are** (Art. 4(1)), so storing them makes Jobscanner a controller with an Art. 14 duty to inform (*"Where personal data have not been obtained from the data subject, the controller shall provide…"*). A tool used solely by one person may fall under Art. 2(2)(c) (*"by a natural person in the course of a purely personal or household activity"*). That exemption disappears if the tool is shared or published. **Policy: strip personal fields at ingestion.**
6. **Technical measures.** Bot walls (HiBob 503, the jpmc Oracle WAF 403) and login flows (`loginFlowRequired=true` on sapsf) are access controls. Getting around them risks claims under unauthorised-access law (PL Criminal Code art. 267: **UNVERIFIED** in this pack) and breaches the anti-circumvention clauses in nearly every ToU. **Never bypass them.**

## 3. Recommended compliant ingestion policy for Jobscanner

1. **Source order:** (a) a documented public API (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, SmartRecruiters, etc., per source-pack.md) → (b) the employer's published feed (Avature RSS, Teamtailor RSS, Personio XML) → (c) a robots-allowed sitemap plus JSON-LD or microdata on the job page → (d) link-only (a web-search hit stored as title + URL + snippet, with no fetch of the target).
2. **robots.txt:** fetch it before the first request to each host and re-fetch every 24 h. Obey `Disallow` for `*` and for our own UA token, and obey `crawl-delay` (careers.icims.com: 5). If robots.txt is 404, use the most conservative limits below. Never request disallowed apply, login, talent-community, `*qtvc=`, `/services/` or `/refreshFacet/` paths.
3. **User agent:** an honest, dedicated string, e.g. `JobscannerBot/0.1 (+https://<project page>; contact: <email>; personal non-commercial job search)`. Never spoof a browser UA; SAP's ToU explicitly forbids "Spoofing user agent strings". Note that the probes for this pack used a browser-like UA with `Jobscanner-personal/0.1` appended. Switch to the dedicated UA in production.
4. **Rate and frequency:** at most **1 request every 5 s per host**, 1 concurrent connection per host, and at most **200 job-detail fetches per host per day**. Refresh sitemaps and feeds **once a day** (or every 12 h for top-priority boards). Fetch detail pages only for new URLs or a changed `lastmod`. For Workday cxs use the tenant's public search with `searchText` (e.g. "Poland", "Engineering Manager"), `limit` ≤ 20, and no more than 5 pages per tenant per day. On 429/503 back off exponentially (×2, cap 24 h). After 3 consecutive 403s, mark the host link-only.
5. **Caching:** use conditional requests (`If-None-Match` / `If-Modified-Since`). Cache robots.txt for 24 h, sitemaps and feeds for 12–24 h, and parsed jobs until they disappear from the sitemap or feed, then delete them within 7 days. Don't keep full HTML. Store only the normalized fields (title, location, remote, department, employment type, posted date, apply URL, a short description excerpt).
6. **Attribution and link-back:** every result shows the employer name and the source host ("via jobs.gft.com") and links to the **canonical job URL** (JSON-LD `url` or `<link rel=canonical>`). Applying always happens on the source site. Don't republish full descriptions; show at most a ~300-character excerpt. This keeps Jobscanner a finder rather than a substitute, which is the key factor under CV-Online.
7. **Personal data:** drop recruiter names, e-mails, phone numbers and photos at parse time (JSON-LD `hiringOrganization` is fine; drop any person fields). Keep usage personal (Art. 2(2)(c)).
8. **AI signals:** where a host signals `ai-input=no`, `ai-train=no` or a ToU TDM reservation (Phenom's own site, SAP, Siemens), don't send its job text to the LLM. Use title and URL only. The LLM should expand *queries*, not ingest third-party pages, by default.
9. **Per-platform switches:** link-only for Oracle CE, Taleo, legacy SF hosts, jobs.sap.com, HiBob, careers.phenom.com and Siemens job detail pages. Allowed with limits for Workday, CSB employer sites, Phenom employer domains, iCIMS (conservative: crawl-delay 5 s and daily), Avature RSS, Jobvite and eRecruiter-employer pages.
10. **Kill switch:** honour any cease request or explicit block within 24 h. Keep a per-host log of requests, so we can show volumes if ever asked.

## 5. Biggest uncertainties
1. **Whether browsewrap vendor ToU bind** a visitor to an employer-branded career site, especially iCIMS (scope covers `*.icims.com`) and Oracle (linked in the footer). No PL court ruling on job-site browsewrap was checked.
2. **Database right for employer career sites.** CV-Online makes indexing a whole job database risky where the maker's investment is harmed. BHB suggests employers' self-created ads may not qualify at all. Courts haven't applied this to ATS-hosted career sites.
3. **Whether Jobscanner's use counts as TDM** (art. 26³ / 8a, DSM Art. 4) or as plain reproduction, and whether natural-language ToU reservations count as "machine-readable".
4. **Workday cxs:** undocumented, not robots-disallowed, and possibly arguable as a "Workday API". The sitemap cap of 100 is observed, not documented.
5. **Oracle REST "internal use only"** and Taleo RSS weren't explored further, by design.
6. **Coverage gaps:** no PL employer found on Taleo, and the Taleo example employers weren't identified. HiBob couldn't be inspected because of the bot wall. EUR-Lex, curia and BAILII blocked direct fetches, so the CJEU wording comes from search-indexed copies and AC&R.
